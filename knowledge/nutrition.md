# Fuelling and hydration

Only for prescriptions and briefings of sessions over 75 minutes, or in heat. The coach advises on
food and drink around sessions; supplements and medical questions go to a professional.

- Carbohydrate during exercise follows duration: under 45 min, none needed; 45 to 75 min, small
  amounts or a mouth rinse; 1 to 2 h, up to 30 g per hour; 2 to 3 h, up to 60 g per hour; beyond
  2.5 h, up to 90 g per hour, only as glucose with fructose and only for a gut trained to it.
  [evidence: Jeukendrup 2014; Thomas 2016]
- The athlete's own tolerance, when their notes give it, sets the upper limit: never prescribe more
  than they have tolerated. [convention]
- Spread intake from the first half hour, in small regular doses, rather than in one go. [convention]
- Before a long or hard session, a meal rich in carbohydrate one to four hours before.
  [evidence: Thomas 2016]
- Drink to limit body-mass loss to under 2 % of body weight; roughly 0.4 to 0.8 litre per hour,
  more in heat, with sodium on long or very sweaty sessions. There is no universal volume.
  [evidence: Sawka 2007]
- When the next hard session is less than a day away, eat carbohydrate and protein soon after a
  long or hard one. [evidence: Thomas 2016]
- Heat raises heart rate at the same power or pace: in heat, judge effort by power, pace or feel
  rather than by heart rate, and drink more. [evidence: Sawka 2007]
