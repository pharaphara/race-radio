# Zones and field tests

- Zones come from a threshold measured in the field, not from maximum heart rate. [convention]
- Cycling: seven zones as fractions of threshold power (Friel); about three quarters of threshold is
  the top of endurance. Running: paces relative to threshold pace (Daniels); easy running is roughly
  50 to 70 s/km slower than threshold. Heart-rate zones as fractions of lactate threshold heart rate.
  [convention: Friel, Daniels]
- Swimming: zones are offsets from critical swim speed, in seconds per 100 m. [convention]
- Tests, offered only when they add value (thresholds stale for 6 to 8 weeks, or perceived effort
  no longer matching the zones), and never during illness or injury:
  - Bike: 20 minutes all out after a proper warm-up, threshold power = 95% of the average.
    [convention]
  - Run: 30 minutes all out; average heart rate of the last 20 minutes = threshold heart rate,
    average pace = threshold pace. [convention]
  - Swim: 400 m then 200 m all out, about ten minutes apart; critical swim speed = 200 m divided by
    the difference of the two times. Critical speed from distance-time trials tracks the speed at a
    blood lactate of about 4 mmol/l. [evidence: Wakayoshi 1992]
- A threshold changes only with the athlete's confirmation. Several estimates (device, Intervals.icu,
  a test) are compared before proposing one, and never mixed. [convention]
