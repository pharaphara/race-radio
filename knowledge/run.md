# Running and trail

- On flat ground, pace measures effort. Uphill it does not: the energy cost of running rises
  steeply with gradient, so hilly runs are read with grade-adjusted pace, running power or heart
  rate, and climbing speed. [evidence: Minetti 2002]
- When the watch ran the structured workout, its laps are the session's structure; on hills pace
  alone cannot tell an uphill rep from a fast flat warm-up. [convention]
- Stride: speed is stride length times cadence. Gaining speed by lengthening the stride rather
  than raising cadence, with a rising vertical ratio, is the pattern to flag; always compare at
  similar paces, since stride lengthens naturally with speed. [convention]
- Trail load is not judged on distance; climbing changes everything. [convention]
